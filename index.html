What this engine is — and what it can never be (read once, it matters)

This app does not predict prices — it cannot. It ranks these ~28 stocks against each other from past price data on a free, delayed feed, then applies fixed rules to suggest a default trade and a size. A high score means "strongest recent price trend in this list," never "will go up." Every number here is a mechanical default, not a forecast, not advice, and not a guarantee.

This strategy deliberately concentrates and chases volatility because the contest pays only 1st place. Concentration widens your range of outcomes in both directions: it raises your chance of finishing 1st and your chance of finishing last, and the single most likely result is still neither. That trade-off is correct only because 2nd place pays the same as last. Following these rules perfectly can still finish you last — that is an expected outcome, not a bug.

Stops are checked once per daily scan and are decision thresholds, not guaranteed exits; an overnight or earnings gap can blow straight through them, so a real loss can be far larger than the stated stop %. The ticker list was picked in 2026 with hindsight about which names already ran, so that choice — not the scoring math — may be the biggest driver of results. Fake money only. Using this logic with real money would be reckless. Educational tool, no licensed advisor involved.

Daily routine (9 steps)
  1. Open once per trading day, after the market opens (pre-market data reads as zeros).
  2. Run the scan and confirm it says complete — a partial scan makes every ranking unreliable.
  3. Keep your rank and gap-to-leader current. From Feb 2027 they set the whole risk posture.
  4. Holdings first: do every SELL and TRIM before looking at any buys.
  5. Then the buy list: enter at the shown price, the shown share count, and note the stop.
  6. "Earnings in Xd" tags are coin flips on direction — don't oversize them.
  7. Skip any card flagged low-confidence unless nothing else qualifies.
  8. Do nothing with HOLDs. Overtrading is how accounts bleed.
  9. If it says hold cash, hold cash. Never force a trade.

Trade Journal

Every trade with your reasoning — your track record for the writeup.

The one thing that changes everything: you're not investing, you're competing.

In real investing you want the best return for the risk you take. In a class contest, risk-adjusted return is worthless. Second place pays the same as last. The only thing that matters is finishing #1 in your class by March 2027. That single fact flips the optimal strategy on its head.

If everyone in the class roughly tracks the market, the winner is whoever took the biggest concentrated bet that happened to pay off. The math rewards variance, not safety. A diversified, sensible portfolio is how you guarantee a middle-of-the-pack finish.

Your plan for the ~7-month window

  • Concentrate. 3–5 high-conviction names, not 20. Every extra name you add pulls you closer to the class average.
  • Tilt toward high beta + catalysts. High-volatility names and stocks with upcoming earnings give you the big moves you need. The watchlist tags beta and earnings dates for exactly this.
  • Keep 20–40% dry powder early. You have months. Don't blow the whole $1M in week one — leave room to press winners and pounce on catalysts.
  • Size by conviction, but cap the downside. Biggest conviction = biggest position, but keep any single entry under ~25–30% so one blowup doesn't end your run with months still on the clock.

Risk rules that create the winning shape

  • Cut losers fast (mental stop ~ -20 to -25%). A broken thesis is a broken thesis. Don't average down to "get back to even."
  • Let winners run. The whole game is manufacturing one or two fat winners. Trimming them early kills the right tail you're counting on.
  • Respect earnings. They're the biggest single-day moves you'll see — your best friend and worst enemy. Size for the volatility, don't get caught by surprise.

Endgame (Feb–March 2027)

  • If you're ahead: de-risk and protect the lead. Shift toward cash / lower beta. You don't need variance anymore.
  • If you're behind: the contest math says concentrate harder. You need a big swing, not a safe one. Uncomfortable, but it's the correct play when second = last.
Honesty note, because this is a school project and the point is to learn: everything above is contest-optimal, not life-optimal. With your real money you'd diversify, keep costs low, and hold for years. The concentrated, high-variance approach is correct here only because the payoff structure is winner-take-all. Say that in your writeup — it shows you actually understand risk, not just that you got lucky.
No tool, this one included, can tell you which stocks will go up. The "signals" in the watchlist are simple rules based on public data (momentum, volatility, earnings timing) — decision support, not predictions. Anyone who promises you guaranteed buy/sell calls is selling something.
